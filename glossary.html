Glossary

The DC power glossary

Short, plain definitions. Type to filter.

AC (alternating current)
Current that reverses direction many times a second (50 or 60 Hz). Used by grids and most household appliances.
Absorption stage
The charging phase where a charger holds a constant voltage while current tapers as the battery fills.
Amp (A)
Unit of electric current — the rate of charge flowing through a conductor.
Amp-hour (Ah)
Battery capacity: one amp for one hour. Multiply by voltage to get watt-hours.
Ampacity
The maximum continuous current a conductor can carry without exceeding its temperature rating.
AWG (American Wire Gauge)
Wire size system where smaller numbers mean thicker wire; 4/0 is very thick, 18 is thin.
Balancing
BMS function that keeps individual cells at matching voltage so the pack can use its full capacity.
BMS (battery management system)
Electronics that monitor and protect a lithium battery: voltage, current, temperature and cell balance.
Busbar
A solid conductor that distributes high current to several cables neatly and safely.
C-rate
Charge or discharge current relative to capacity; 1C empties a battery in one hour, 0.5C in two.
CCS (Combined Charging System)
EV connector family that adds two DC pins below an AC plug: CCS1 in North America, CCS2 in Europe and much of the world.
Charge controller
Device that regulates solar panel output to charge a battery safely. See MPPT and PWM.
Charging curve
A vehicle's charging power plotted against state of charge; power typically tapers as the battery fills.
CHAdeMO
A DC fast-charging connector standard from Japan, used by some older EVs elsewhere.
Circular mil (cmil)
Unit of wire cross-sectional area used in voltage-drop calculations.
Clipping
Lost solar output when panel DC power exceeds what the inverter can convert.
DC (direct current)
Current that flows in one direction. Produced by solar cells, stored in batteries, used by electronics and EV packs.
DC-DC charger
Converts one DC voltage to another, e.g. charging a house battery from a vehicle alternator.
DC fast charger (DCFC)
Off-board charger that supplies DC straight to an EV battery, typically 50–400+ kW.
DC/AC ratio
Solar array DC rating divided by inverter AC rating; slightly above 1 is common.
Degradation
Gradual loss of capacity (batteries) or output (solar panels) over time.
Depth of discharge (DoD)
How much of a battery's capacity is used in a cycle. 80% DoD means discharging to 20% state of charge.
Efficiency (EV)
Distance per unit of energy, e.g. miles per kWh or Wh per km.
Float charge
A low maintenance voltage after full charge; lead-acid needs it, LiFePO4 often does not.
Fuse
A sacrificial link that opens on over-current to protect wiring from overheating.
Ground fault
An unintended current path to ground; can be dangerous in PV arrays and must be detected.
Hybrid inverter
An inverter that manages solar input, battery charging and AC output in one unit.
Interrupt rating (AIC)
The maximum fault current a fuse or breaker can safely stop.
Inverter
Converts DC to AC. Rated by continuous and surge watts.
J1772
North American AC charging connector (Type 1), used for Level 1 and Level 2.
J3400 (NACS)
SAE standard for the North American Charging Standard connector, carrying AC and DC through one compact plug.
kW vs kWh
kW is power (rate); kWh is energy (amount). A 10 kW charger running one hour delivers 10 kWh.
Level 1 / Level 2
AC charging at 120 V (≈1.9 kW) or 208–240 V (≈7–19 kW) in North America.
LFP / LiFePO4
Lithium iron phosphate chemistry: long cycle life, thermally stable, 3.2 V per cell.
Microinverter
A small inverter on each solar panel, converting to AC at the roof.
MPPT
Maximum power point tracking: a charge controller that adjusts voltage and current to extract the most power from panels.
Net metering
Billing arrangement crediting exported solar energy against energy you buy; rules vary widely.
NMC
Nickel manganese cobalt lithium chemistry: high energy density, used in many EVs.
Ohm (Ω)
Unit of resistance. Voltage = current × resistance.
Onboard charger (OBC)
Converter inside an EV that turns AC from a Level 1/2 station into DC for the battery.
Peak sun hours
Daily solar energy expressed as hours of 1,000 W/m² sunlight; used to size arrays.
Preconditioning
Heating or cooling an EV battery before fast charging so it can accept more power.
PWM controller
Simpler, cheaper solar controller; less efficient than MPPT when panel and battery voltages differ.
Round-trip efficiency
Energy you get out of a battery divided by energy you put in.
Series / parallel
Series adds voltage; parallel adds capacity (amp-hours).
Shunt
Precision resistor used by battery monitors to measure current and track state of charge.
State of charge (SoC)
How full a battery is, as a percentage.
State of health (SoH)
A battery's current capacity compared with when new.
String (PV)
Panels wired in series to raise voltage; residential strings can exceed 400 V DC.
Surge rating
Short-term power an inverter can deliver to start motors and compressors.
Taper
The reduction in charging current as a battery approaches full.
V2H / V2G / V2L
Vehicle-to-home, vehicle-to-grid and vehicle-to-load: using an EV battery to power a house, the grid or devices.
Volt (V)
Unit of electrical potential difference — the "pressure" pushing current.
Voltage drop
Voltage lost along a cable due to its resistance; keep it under about 3% for most DC circuits.
Watt (W) / watt-hour (Wh)
Power and energy. Watts = volts × amps; watt-hours = watts × hours.
