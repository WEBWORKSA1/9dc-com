DC-coupled vs AC-coupled solar + storage

9DC Editorial · Updated October 2026 · 8 min read

Solar panels produce direct current. Your appliances and the grid run on alternating current. Batteries store direct current. Every solar-plus-storage system is a decision about where — and how many times — energy crosses between DC and AC.

The three conversions

Each conversion between DC and AC wastes a few percent as heat. Modern inverters are efficient — often 95–98% at good operating points — but losses stack when energy is converted more than once.

  • PV → home: DC from panels is inverted to AC.
  • PV → battery: DC either goes straight in (DC-coupled) or is inverted to AC and then rectified back to DC (AC-coupled).
  • Battery → home: DC is inverted to AC.

DC-coupled systems

Panels feed a charge controller (usually MPPT) that charges the battery on the DC side. A single hybrid inverter converts DC to AC for the home. Solar stored in the battery is converted once on the way in (DC–DC) and once on the way out.

Strengths: fewer conversions for stored energy; one inverter instead of two; can capture "clipped" solar when the array is larger than the inverter; natural fit for off-grid and new builds.

Trade-offs: battery, inverter and charge controller usually need to be compatible or from one ecosystem; retrofitting to an existing grid-tied system can mean replacing the inverter.

AC-coupled systems

The solar array keeps its own inverter (string or microinverters). A separate battery system with its own inverter/charger connects on the AC side. Solar headed for storage is inverted to AC, then converted back to DC to charge the battery, then inverted again when used.

Strengths: easy to add to an existing solar system; battery and solar can be from different brands; batteries can be placed away from the panels.

Trade-offs: a few percent more round-trip loss for solar that passes through the battery; two inverters to buy and maintain.

How to choose

  1. Already have solar? AC coupling is usually the simplest, cheapest path to storage.
  2. Building new and want backup? A DC-coupled hybrid system is usually more efficient.
  3. Off-grid? DC coupling with a properly sized battery bank is the default. Start with our battery bank sizer.
  4. Want EV charging too? Check whether the system can supply a Level 2 charger during an outage and how much continuous power the inverter allows.
Ask your installer: continuous and surge output of the inverter in backup mode, which circuits are backed up, the warranty on the battery's usable energy (kWh) and cycles, and how the system behaves when the grid goes down.
